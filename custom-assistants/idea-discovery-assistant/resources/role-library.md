# AI Role Library

Common roles for custom AI assistants, collected by the AI Test Kitchen at Boston College. Each role names one kind of help an assistant can give, with a few example assistants that people have built in that role.

## How to use this library

The roles are starting points, not a menu. Most good assistants start from one role and get reshaped for a specific context, or borrow from two or three.

**If you're an AI assistant using this as reference:** start from the person's need, not the list. Once you know what they're trying to do, mention the two or three roles closest to it, then help them shape their own version: a name that fits their context, its one job, what it does, and what it should not do.

**If you're a person:** skim the roles, then attach this file to a conversation with any AI tool, or paste it in, and shape a role for your own work. Start whichever way fits:

- **Not sure where to start?** Ask the AI to interview you about your work, one question at a time.
- **Have a loose idea?** Give it a messy brain dump and ask it to say back what it heard.
- **Have context or materials?** Share them (public or made-up information only) and ask which roles fit and how you'd adapt one.

## Process or product?

Every role below is marked one of two ways:

- **Process-oriented:** the value is in the conversation. The assistant helps someone think, practice, or reflect, and often shouldn't hand over answers. "Walk me through this."
- **Product-oriented:** the value is in the output: a draft, a plan, a summary, cleaned-up data. "Give me a draft of this."

Many real assistants mix both. Deciding which matters most shapes the instructions: a process assistant needs to know what to ask and when to hold back, and a product assistant needs a clear picture of a good result.

## Questions for shaping a role

- Who is it for: just you, or other people?
- What is its one job?
- Is it mostly process or mostly product?
- What does it need to know to do the job well?
- What should it never do?

## Process-oriented roles

### Content Navigator
Helps people explore a collection of content and find information they can use.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Finding information
- Example assistants: Course Assistant Chatbot, Narrative Journalism Textbook Guide

### Learning Tutor
Helps learners do more than they could on their own, without doing the work for them.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Scaffolding
- Example assistants: Stuckbot, General Tutor, Vector Explorer, GAIL (GenAI Literacy Assistant)

### Skills Trainer
Facilitates focused, repeated practice to build specific skills and work toward mastery.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Mastery practice
- Example assistants: Deconstructing/Constructing Texts, Practice Problem Generator (Regression Class), Practice Problem Generator (Intro Statistics), PolicymakerGPT

### Feedback Facilitator
Provides timely, actionable feedback to help people understand their strengths and areas for growth.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Formative feedback
- Example assistants: AI Mentor Gives Feedback, Portfolio Partner, Coach Capstone

### Scenario Simulator
Guides people through role-plays and case studies so they can apply skills in realistic situations.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Experiential learning
- Example assistants: Simulation Creator, Negotiation Simulator, AI Student

### Process Coach
Walks people through a complex process that needs thoughtful input across multiple steps.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Reducing cognitive load
- Example assistants: Research Problem Statement Process Coach, Group Project Process Coach, Team Charter, Marlowe Thesis Drafting Coach, Legal Research Process Coach, AI Course Policy Assistant, Meta Assistant: Guide for Building AI Assistants, Course Blueprint Writer

### Reflective Guide
Supports people in exploring their own experiences and becoming more aware of how they learn.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Metacognition and self-awareness
- Example assistants: Stuckbot, AI Working Group Exit Ticket, Pre-Class Questionnaire, Class Reflection Aid, Team After Action Review, Being Whole Reflective Assistant, Idea Discovery Assistant

### Creative Partner
Helps people brainstorm when they're stuck and develop their thinking beyond the first good idea.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Creative thinking
- Example assistants: Idea Discovery Assistant

### Socratic Questioner
Engages people in dialogue that helps them question assumptions and test their ideas.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Critical thinking
- Example assistants: Devil's Advocate

### Decision Strategist
Helps weigh pros and cons, assess risks, and evaluate options to support a well-informed decision.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Strategic thinking
- Example assistants: Choosing the Write Path for You

### Input Interviewer
Collects information from people in an interactive format that adapts to their answers.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Gathering feedback and input
- Example assistants: AI Working Group Exit Ticket, Pre-Class Questionnaire

### Troubleshooting Consultant
Helps people diagnose and solve technical problems with clear guidance and relevant background.
- Type: Process-oriented
- Focus: Tech support

## Product-oriented roles

### Planning Architect
Helps plan and organize projects or events, breaking them into clear steps to keep everything on track.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Making plans
- Example assistants: Team Premortem

### Learning Designer
Helps create engaging, effective learning experiences tailored to the varied needs of students.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Teaching support
- Example assistants: Teaching Assistant Blueprint, Syllabus Co-creator, Structured Prompt Designer, Quiz Creator, Project Ideas for Class, Lesson Crafter, Co-develop an Explanation, Active Learning Co-creator, Course Blueprint Writer

### Data Wrangler
Organizes and cleans data so it's well structured, accurate, and ready for analysis.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Data preparation

### Research Assistant
Helps gather, organize, and present relevant information to support purposeful action.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Guided research

### Editorial Assistant
Helps improve and manage written content for individuals and groups.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Communication
- Example assistants: Personal Copy Editor

### Critical Reviewer
Offers clear, focused feedback that improves quality and clarity and keeps a document aligned with its goals.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Aligned evaluation

### Insight Gatherer
Pulls together information from different sources into clear summaries and actionable insights.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Synthesis

### Data Analyst
Turns raw data into usable knowledge by analyzing key measures and presenting findings in an accessible format.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Data-driven insights

### Code Monkey
Supports writing, debugging, and refining code, with tips that fill knowledge gaps quickly.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Programming

### Content Transformer
Reworks content for different purposes, perspectives, audiences, or levels of complexity.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Repurposing content

### Structured Creator
Partners with authors to generate content, creatively and efficiently, that follows an established format.
- Type: Product-oriented
- Focus: Content development
- Example assistants: AI Role Developer
